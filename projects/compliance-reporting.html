The reporting problem

A dashboard can look current even when its extract is incomplete, stale or late. This project separates case performance from the health of the reporting process so that both can be reviewed.

Approach

  1. Joined cases to departments in MySQL 8.4 and produced 84 daily, 12 weekly and four monthly reporting rows.
  2. Defined backlog at the period end, elapsed calendar-day resolution time and a due-cohort on-time rate that keeps open overdue cases in the denominator.
  3. Built an Excel case register, formula-driven summary, native department PivotTable and weekly backlog chart. Python validation checks keys, reference values, dates and freshness before publication.

FINDING → NEXT ACTION

157 of 266 open cases are overdue (59.0%). Warehouse has the highest open backlog at 99 cases, including 56 overdue. The next step would be a review of ageing, case mix and ownership with the relevant department leads.

Prioritise an ageing review, not an unsupported claim that one department is underperforming. The generator deliberately slows some Warehouse resolutions; the example does not establish a real-world cause.

Verification

Queries were executed in MySQL 8.4.11. All headline, department and 100 period-row outputs reconcile to independent Python calculations. Nineteen tests pass. Invalid and stale runs cannot replace the last successful report; valid late runs are flagged. Native Pivot structure and an independent workbook recalculation were checked.

Scope and limitations

Fictional data covering 29 June-20 September 2026. June and September are partial months. There is no production scheduler, email delivery or live data connection. Desktop Excel refresh remains unverified. Scenario evaluation times are simulated and are distinct from actual execution timestamps.
