01 / SUPPLIER PERFORMANCEPOWER BI

Are suppliers delivering
on their promises?

A two-page report brings delivery reliability, receipt lead times and overdue orders into one view. Supplier, item category and due-month filters support follow-up on delivery problems.

What I built

A four-table model with three one-to-many relationships and eight DAX measures. The OTIF calculation includes overdue, unreceived lines in its denominator so incomplete deliveries are not hidden.

What the data says

Only 575 of 1,488 due lines arrived on time and in full. This gives a clear starting point for supplier reviews; it does not establish the causes of missed delivery promises.

Definitions, verification and limitations

OTIF: order lines due by 21 September 2026, received by their due date, with received quantity at least the ordered quantity, divided by all lines due by that date. This is line-weighted, not value-weighted. The source assumes one receipt record per line.

Verification: the supplied Windows validation record reports successful refresh, both report pages rendered, supplier selection and clearing, and the saved report with an embedded data model on 22 September 2026. This packaging review did not repeat native Power BI Desktop testing on the Mac.

Refresh: the included PBIP source uses a local DataFolder parameter. Point it to the extracted clean-data folder before refreshing. There is no deployed Power BI Service report or scheduled cloud refresh.

02 / INVENTORY PLANNINGEXCEL

Know what to replenish.
Show the assumptions.

An inventory workbook compares stock position with demand, lead time and safety-stock rules. It flags the items that need attention and estimates the purchase cost of replenishment.

What I built

Exact-match VLOOKUPs, SUMIFS and COUNTIFS connect item and supplier attributes to an 80-item inventory model. Excel Tables, validation rules, conditional formatting and a native chart make the calculations easier to inspect.

A decision to investigate

27 items meet the reorder rule. The model proposes £766,792 in purchases; this is a scenario estimate for review, not an approved spend or a claimed cost saving.

Turnover is annualised from a 90-day demand-cost proxy and average stock value. Inventory and order data share item IDs but are not a reconciled stock ledger.

Replenishment logic and workbook limitations

Stock position = on hand − allocated + on order. Daily demand = trailing 90-day demand ÷ 90. The reorder point covers lead time plus safety days; the target also covers the review cycle. An order is proposed only when stock position is at or below the reorder point.

The model does not include minimum order quantities, pack sizes, on-order arrival timing or seasonality. Formula summaries are used, not a verified native PivotTable. The workbook is designed for the supplied 80-item fixture; adding items requires extending dependent ranges and checking calculations. Cached formula values and chart structure were checked; native Excel recalculation was not verified during packaging.

03 / REPORTING CONTROLSPYTHON

Stop bad data before
it becomes a report.

A repeatable validation pipeline checks purchase orders, item and supplier references, and bills of materials before promoting a new report as the latest trusted version.

What I built

CSV diagnostics, accepted-record outputs, JSON summaries and HTML reports. A content-based run ID makes repeated runs reproducible. Failed runs retain their diagnostic evidence without replacing the last successful report pointer.

Evidence, not just a diagram

The intentionally dirty fixture triggers nine issues and rejects five order rows. Twelve tests cover date boundaries, partial receipts, invalid references, BOM cycles and publication protection.

Clean and dirty inputs are separate fixtures. This pipeline detects and quarantines issues; it does not automatically repair the dirty data.

Process design and implementation boundary

Manual process being modelled: export → copy and paste → reconcile → prepare report → email. The proposed workflow validates the export first, records failures, then makes successful outputs available for approved delivery.

Implemented: a local Python pipeline, data checks and tests. Proposed: a Power Automate delivery flow. There is no live flow, scheduler, email integration or SAP connection, and no measured time-saving claim. ERP/MRP familiarity here means working with simulated purchase-order, item, supplier and BOM structures.
